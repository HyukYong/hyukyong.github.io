What is inside

Five things it does that a textbook cannot.

Chembook is not a PDF with a search box. Each of these is interactive because the underlying data is real — precomputed quantum chemistry, shipped in the download and read from your device.

01 / REACTIONS

Step through the mechanism, not a diagram.

Ninety named organic mechanisms, frame by frame. Every structure is a real geometry — most of them quantum-optimized — with curved electron-pushing arrows drawn on the actual atoms rather than sketched over a picture. Toggle any reaction between the 3D model and a clean 2D skeletal depiction.

90 mechanismsQM geometriesElectron arrows2D ⇄ 3D

02 / ORBITALS

Orbitals you can turn in your hand.

Real computed isosurfaces rendered from volumetric data, not illustrations — s, p, d and f atomic orbitals through to molecular orbitals, with an energy ladder you tap to render each level and an isovalue control to thin or thicken the surface.

Computed isosurfacesMO ladderIsovalue control

03 / VIBRATIONS

Watch the molecule move on each band.

Computed normal modes animated on the structure itself — pick a band and see exactly which stretch or bend it belongs to, with its symmetry label and whether it is IR or Raman active. The step between a spectrum and the motion behind it, which a printed table can only assert.

Animated normal modesSymmetry labelsIR / Raman activity

04 / DYNAMICS

A simulation, not an animation.

Argon condensing, water freezing into an ice lattice and melting again, salt dissolving in a polar solvent. Velocity Verlet with a Langevin thermostat, temperature and density under your control, and the radial distribution function redrawing as it runs.

Lennard-JonesTIP4P waterLive g(r)Phase detection

05 / QUIZ

Recall practice that is actually chemistry.

Six modes: drag elements into the periodic table, draw compounds on a Lewis word-search grid against the clock, classify reaction mechanisms and predict the product, identify a group from its IR or NMR, name a vibration you are watching, and recall amino acids by structure and code. Scored, timed, and drawn from the same data as the reference side of the app.

Offline by construction

Every mechanism, orbital cube, spectrum and reference table ships inside the download. The app makes no network requests at all — not for content, not for analytics, not for crash reports. It works the same on a plane, in a basement lab, or on a phone in airplane mode for a week.

That is also why there is nothing to collect. Chembook has no account, no sign-in, and no server. Your progress, bookmarks and quiz results are files in the app's own storage on your device, and Settings → Reset all data erases them.

We have declared Data Not Collected on the App Store, and the privacy manifest inside the app says the same. Read the Chembook privacy policy.
